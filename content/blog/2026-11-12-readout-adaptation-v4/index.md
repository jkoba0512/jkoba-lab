Version 4 of our bioRxiv preprint is now available under the title “Readout adaptation in simulated arm reaching: learned compensation and performance after updates stop.”

The revised study compares six readout-adaptation implementations using continuous-time network, echo-state, and radial-basis features. It measures both the learned torque correction and the performance retained when weight updates stop. All six implementations improve tracking over their fixed-readout baselines and meet the study's after-effect criterion in every evaluation run. With updates stopped, the final readouts reduce tracking error by 40–97% relative to their pre-adaptation versions.

The comparison describes complete implementations under their tested preparation and hyperparameter settings; it does not isolate feature representation or update rule as the sole cause of performance differences.

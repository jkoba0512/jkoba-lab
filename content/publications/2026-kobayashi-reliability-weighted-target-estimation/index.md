abstract: "Simulated target-position estimation compared recalibration, learned fixed weights, input-dependent weighting, and methods using longer observation histories. Each two-input method combined a current measurement with the mean of three initial measurements, learned from noisy reference measurements, and received no true target positions. Calibration, training, validation, and independent evaluation were repeated 100 times in each of four error conditions. Recalibration and fixed-weight learning explained most of the improvement under reduced measurement accuracy and temporal correlation. With occasional large measurement errors, input-dependent weighting reduced mean squared error by 4.05% relative to learned fixed weights; its additional benefit was below 0.5% under independent errors and reduced current accuracy. Fixed weights had slightly lower mean error under temporal correlation, and longer observation histories performed better in several conditions. Shared coordinate bias and correlated reference errors limit conclusions about physical accuracy."
